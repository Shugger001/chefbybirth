What is Kenkey?

Kenkey (also called komi or dokon) is a fermented corn dough staple from Ghana. Corn is soaked, ground, partially cooked, then fermented for days until it develops its signature tangy flavor.

The dough is wrapped in corn husks or plantain leaves and steamed into firm balls. It's traditionally eaten with fried fish, pepper sauce (shito), and fresh vegetables — a complete, satisfying meal rooted in West African home cooking.

  • 3-day fermentation — develops authentic sour tang
  • Leaf-wrapped — steamed in corn husks for aroma
  • Gluten-free — made from corn, naturally wheat-free
